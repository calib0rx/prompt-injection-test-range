Red Team Operations: Planning and Execution

Offensive Security · February 2026

Red teaming is the practice of simulating real-world attacks against your own organization to identify security gaps before adversaries do. Unlike penetration testing, which typically focuses on specific systems or applications, red teaming evaluates the organization's overall detection and response capabilities against realistic threat scenarios.

Scope and Rules of Engagement

Every red team engagement starts with a clear scope and rules of engagement document. This defines what is in bounds, what is off limits, and what communication channels exist for emergencies. The rules of engagement protect both the red team and the organization from unintended consequences — a red team operation that causes a production outage has failed regardless of what vulnerabilities it discovered.

Assumed Breach Scenarios

The most valuable red team exercises start from an assumed breach position. Rather than spending weeks on initial access (which tests perimeter defenses, not internal security), the red team is given a foothold — a compromised workstation, a set of valid credentials — and tasked with achieving specific objectives: accessing crown jewel data, establishing persistence, or demonstrating lateral movement to critical systems. This approach maximizes the signal from each engagement.
