Concentration Risk

The most underappreciated supply chain risk is concentration. When multiple tiers of your supply chain converge on a single geography, facility, or vendor, you have a single point of failure that may not be visible from your direct supplier relationships. Mapping your supply chain to at least tier three is essential for identifying these hidden dependencies.

Software Supply Chain

Software supply chain attacks have grown from a niche concern to a board-level risk. The SolarWinds compromise demonstrated that even well-resourced organizations can be breached through trusted software update channels. The response has been an industry-wide push toward software bills of materials (SBOMs), build provenance attestation, and dependency pinning. These measures reduce risk but do not eliminate it — the attack surface of modern software dependency trees is simply too large for complete coverage.

Contractual Protections

Contracts remain the primary mechanism for managing supplier risk, yet most procurement teams negotiate contracts without input from security or risk management. At minimum, contracts with critical suppliers should include right-to-audit clauses, breach notification timelines, business continuity requirements, and data handling obligations. These provisions are only valuable if they are exercised — an audit right you never use provides zero risk reduction.
