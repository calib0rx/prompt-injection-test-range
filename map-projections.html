Choosing the Right Map Projection for Your Project

Geodata Field Notes · February 2026

Every map projection distorts the Earth's surface in some way — the only question is which properties you preserve and which you sacrifice. The choice of projection affects area calculations, distance measurements, shape fidelity, and visual aesthetics. There is no universally correct projection, only projections that are appropriate for specific use cases.

Conformal Projections

Conformal projections preserve local angles and shapes at the cost of area distortion. The Mercator projection is conformal, which is why it works well for navigation — bearing lines are straight — but exaggerates areas at high latitudes. Equal-Area Projections

Equal-area projections preserve relative area at the cost of shape distortion. Albers Equal Area Conic is the standard for thematic maps of mid-latitude countries — USGS uses it for the National Atlas. Mollweide and Goode Homolosine are common choices for global equal-area maps. Equidistant Projections

Equidistant projections preserve distance from one or two points to all other points. The Azimuthal Equidistant projection, centered on a point of interest, is used for radio propagation studies and airline route maps. No projection preserves distance between all pairs of points — this is a mathematical impossibility on a flat surface.
