Landsat 9 continues the 50-year Landsat archive at 30-meter resolution. Commercial providers like Planet offer daily 3-meter coverage globally. The challenge has shifted from data availability to data management — choosing the right imagery for the task, handling atmospheric correction, and processing at scale.

Atmospheric Correction

Raw satellite imagery records top-of-atmosphere (TOA) reflectance, which includes atmospheric scattering and absorption. For quantitative analysis — vegetation indices, change detection, classification — surface reflectance (SR) is required. Sentinel-2 Level-2A products include atmospheric correction via Sen2Cor. For Landsat, USGS provides Collection 2 Level-2 products with LaSRC correction. When pre-corrected products are not available, tools like ACOLITE or 6S can perform custom atmospheric correction, but parameterization requires knowledge of local atmospheric conditions.

Cloud Masking

Clouds are the persistent enemy of optical remote sensing. Scene Classification maps (SCL) from Sen2Cor provide pixel-level cloud, cloud shadow, and cirrus masks for Sentinel-2. Quality Assessment bands in Landsat encode similar information. Machine learning approaches like s2cloudless generally outperform threshold-based methods in complex scenes with thin clouds, haze, or bright surfaces that confuse traditional algorithms. Always visually validate cloud masks — over-masking removes valid data, under-masking corrupts analysis.
