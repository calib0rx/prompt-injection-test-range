LiDAR Point Cloud Processing: From Collection to DEM

Geodata Field Notes · January 2026

Airborne LiDAR surveys generate dense point clouds — often hundreds of points per square meter — that capture terrain, vegetation, and built structures with centimeter-level vertical accuracy. Transforming these raw point clouds into usable digital elevation models requires a multi-step pipeline: noise filtering, ground classification, interpolation, and quality assessment.

Ground Classification

Separating ground returns from vegetation, buildings, and noise is the critical step in DEM production. Cloth Simulation Filtering (CSF) is a newer alternative that simulates draping a cloth over an inverted point cloud. Both require parameter tuning based on terrain complexity — steep slopes and dense vegetation are the hardest cases.

Interpolation Methods

Once ground points are classified, they must be interpolated onto a regular grid to produce a raster DEM. Triangulated Irregular Network (TIN) interpolation preserves breaklines and handles variable point density well. Inverse Distance Weighting (IDW) is simpler but tends to produce bull's-eye artifacts around isolated points. For most applications, TIN-based interpolation followed by smoothing produces the best results.

Accuracy Assessment

DEM accuracy should be validated against independent ground truth — typically RTK GPS checkpoints collected on bare ground. The standard metric is RMSE of vertical residuals. ASPRS accuracy standards define thresholds by product class: a 10-cm RMSE DEM requires checkpoints with at most 3-cm vertical accuracy. Without independent validation, the accuracy of a LiDAR-derived DEM is unknown regardless of the sensor specification.
