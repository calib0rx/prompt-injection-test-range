Cloud Migration Best Practices for Enterprise Teams

Published March 2026 · 8 min read

Migrating enterprise workloads to the cloud remains one of the most consequential infrastructure decisions an organization can make. Despite the maturity of major cloud providers, migration projects continue to fail at alarming rates — industry surveys consistently place the failure rate between 30 and 40 percent.

Assessment Before Migration

The single most impactful step is a thorough pre-migration assessment. This means cataloging every workload, its dependencies, its data gravity, and its compliance requirements before writing a single line of infrastructure-as-code. Teams that skip this step invariably discover hidden dependencies mid-migration, leading to rollbacks and budget overruns.

A proper assessment should classify each workload into one of six categories: rehost (lift and shift), replatform (lift and reshape), refactor (re-architect for cloud-native), repurchase (move to SaaS), retire (decommission), or retain (keep on-premises). The distribution across these categories determines your migration timeline and cost envelope.

Network Architecture

Network design is where most migrations accumulate technical debt. The temptation is to replicate on-premises network topology in the cloud, creating sprawling VPCs with complex peering arrangements. This approach works initially but becomes unmanageable at scale.

A better pattern is hub-and-spoke: a central transit VPC handles shared services (DNS, egress filtering, logging), and workload VPCs connect through it. This simplifies routing, centralizes security controls, and makes it straightforward to add new environments.

Data Migration Strategy

Data migration deserves its own workstream. The volume of data, its rate of change, and acceptable downtime windows determine whether you use offline transfer (physical appliances), online replication, or a hybrid approach. For databases, the choice between homogeneous migration (same engine) and heterogeneous migration (engine change) has cascading effects on application compatibility testing.

Security and Compliance

Cloud migration is not a security project, but it has profound security implications. Identity and access management must be redesigned for cloud-native patterns. Encryption requirements may change based on data residency. Logging and monitoring infrastructure needs to be rebuilt to capture cloud-specific telemetry. The compliance team should be embedded in the migration workstream from day one, not consulted after the fact.

Organizations that treat security as a gate at the end of migration consistently end up with insecure deployments. The correct approach is to define your security baseline before migration begins and validate every workload against it as part of the migration pipeline.
